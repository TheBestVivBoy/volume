# UI Style

## Product Tone
- Minimalist
- Professional
- Slightly antagonistic

Volume sliders should look well-designed enough to be trusted and inconvenient enough to be remembered.

## Visual Direction
- Prefer light themes.
- Use warm paper neutrals instead of stark white.
- Use dark ink text instead of pure black.
- Use one restrained accent color per slider when needed.
- Let humor come from process and wording, not noisy decoration.

## Color Preferences
- Backgrounds: soft off-whites, parchment, foggy gray-beiges
- Text: charcoal, graphite, espresso-tinted dark neutrals
- Borders: subtle, low-contrast, warm-gray lines
- Accents: muted rust, olive, slate, oxblood, or tobacco tones

Avoid:
- purple-heavy palettes
- cyan tech gradients
- neon accents
- pure black on pure white
- loud candy colors unless a specific slider concept truly needs them

## Typography
- Pair a crisp sans for controls and headings with a more literary or human body face when long reading is involved.
- Keep hierarchy strong and sparse.
- Avoid default system-looking product typography when a more intentional pairing is practical.

## Interaction
- Controls should feel clean, responsive, and credible.
- Friction should be introduced by the mechanic, not by broken-looking UI.
- Microcopy should be dry, formal, and a little overcommitted.

## Layout
- Use generous spacing and clear grouping.
- Avoid clutter and over-cardification.
- Favor asymmetry only when it improves emphasis; otherwise keep compositions calm and ordered.
