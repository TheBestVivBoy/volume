## Design Context

### Users
People visit Volume to browse and play with intentionally inconvenient volume sliders. The product is a gallery of tiny interaction jokes, so users are here for curiosity, surprise, and the satisfaction of discovering how each slider turns a basic control into a strange little process.

### Brand Personality
Minimalist, professional, annoying.

The site should feel precise and calm on first glance, then mildly unreasonable once the interaction begins. It should present friction with a straight face. The tone is polished, composed, and quietly mischievous rather than loud, chaotic, or meme-heavy.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use a restrained editorial-product aesthetic: light mode, paper-tinted surfaces, dark ink text, subtle warm neutrals, and disciplined spacing. Interfaces should feel like premium productivity tools, policy dashboards, approval forms, or internal systems that take themselves slightly too seriously.

Avoid playful rainbow palettes, gamer aesthetics, neon effects, and overtly goofy visuals. The humor should come from procedural absurdity inside a refined interface.

### Design Principles
1. Make the first impression trustworthy, spare, and competent.
2. Let the inconvenience be the joke, not visual chaos.
3. Use typography and spacing to create authority before the mechanic undermines it.
4. Keep every slider self-contained, readable, and fast to understand.
5. Prefer subtle warmth and material texture over loud decoration.
